Loudspeakers transform electric signals transporting sound information into acoustic waves.
The functioning principle behind them is a coil, upon which a membrane has been set up, is placed within circular magnet generating the acoustic wave from the electric signal applied to the coil.
![[Pasted image 20260511114635.png]]
When an electric signal is applied at the far ends of a conductor, a current consisting of a flow of electrons passes through it, creating a magnetic field which can be concordant or discordant with the magnetic field generated by the magnet, leading to a displacement of the coil.
The coil moving up and down following the polarity causes the movement of the membrane, causing a sound to be emitted.
![[Pasted image 20260511114826.png]]

Each speaker has a resonant frequency, which is the threshold approximation around where the elastic system begins to oscillate.
![[Pasted image 20260511114950.png]]

The efficiency is the effective measurement for the acoustic power of a loudspeaker, so it is its ability to transform electric energy into acoustic energy.
The part of electric energy which doesn't get converted into acoustic energy is dissipated as heat.
Usually a loudspeaker has a very low efficiency, in the range of 1-8%.
At lower frequencies, cone shaped membranes are better than flat ones at gathering and moving air in front of them.
![[Pasted image 20260511115322.png]]

Sensitivity is measured in $\text{dB}_{\text{spl}}$. It quantifies the intensity of sound pressure at one meter distance from the loudspeaker when an electric signal of 1 W is applied to it.
For example, 93 $\text{dB}_{\text{spl}}$/m/W indicates that in the aforementioned conditions a sound pressure of 93 $\text{dB}_{\text{spl}}$ was measured.

Maximum applicable power is measured by 2 quantities:
- Peak power
	- The maximum power the loudspeaker can handle without being damaged. If the signal goes over it, the speaker will be damaged
- RMS (root mean square) power
	- A unit that measures the average applicable power that can be applied for an amount of time before the heat begins to damage the loudspeaker

The differences in the dimensions of the membranes strongly condition the functioning of a loudspeaker. The greater the dimension of the membrane and its mass, the smaller the resonance frequency is (so the better it reproduces the lower frequencies).
We can classify different loudspeaker types based on the size of the membrane:
- Woofer
	- Used for the reproduction of low frequencies
	- The power needed to adequately feed the loudspeaker increases with the increase in air quantity moved by it
- Subwoofer 
	- Used for reproduction of very low frequencies (20-40 Hz)
- Midrange
	- Used for middle frequencies (smaller than woofers)
- Tweeter
	- Loudspeakers with very small membranes
	- Used for high frequencies
